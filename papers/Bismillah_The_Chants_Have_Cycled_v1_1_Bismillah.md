Abstract
This paper examines the lyrical corpus of The Mars Volta (seven studio albums, 2003–2022) through the
interpretive framework developed in two prior papers by the same author: a three-level architecture of
moral responsibility for formed agents (Arfeen, 2026a), and a computational ontology grounded in Islamic
monotheism (Arfeen & Claude, 2026b). The Mars Volta’s lyrics, written primarily by Cedric Bixler-Zavala
across stated inspirations ranging from a friend’s suicide to honour killings to Scientology, recapitulate
structural features the Qur’anic sources also describe: entity–partnership, generational transmission of corrupt
formations, beautification that makes the formed person suppose himself guided, the failure of sanctuary
when the threat is internal, and cyclical closure. The convergence is a Pillar 2 observation (convergence
with an independent domain) rather than a Pillar 1 claim (direct textual evidence), and the analysis is
exploratory rather than confirmatory. The paper does not claim that the lyrics confirm the theological
framework. It proposes that the structural resemblance is non-trivial, subject to the convergence-illusion risk
and the confounds identified in Section 6. The paper also documents how the analysis itself recapitulated a
retracted framework (recorded in Arfeen 2026a, Appendix B) before the replacement architecture corrected
it, and treats that self-correction as part of the result.
Keywords: formed agency, generational transmission, structural convergence, The Mars Volta, Qur’anic
exegesis, computational ontology, adversarial architecture, beautification, innate disposition, independent
articulation

1. Introduction
1.1 The interpretive problem
The Mars Volta’s lyrics are among the most deliberately opaque in contemporary rock. Cedric Bixler-Zavala’s
writing is surrealist, densely metaphorical, and resistant to paraphrase. Across seven studio albums and two
decades, his stated inspirations include a fictionalised narrative drawing on the life and suicide of his friend
Julio Venegas (De-Loused in the Comatorium, 2003), a concept whose origin story was later acknowledged as
fabricated (Frances the Mute, 2005; see Rodriguez-Lopez & Bixler-Zavala, 2017), the abuse and fear of religion
(Amputechture, 2006), a ouija board purchased in Jerusalem (The Bedlam in Goliath, 2008), personal themes
of loss and relationship in a more restrained register (Octahedron, 2009), mythological and nursery-rhyme
sources (Noctourniquet, 2012), and his family’s legal battles with the Church of Scientology (The Mars Volta,
2022; see Beaumont-Thomas, 2022).
Despite these varied inspirations, a recurring structural pattern appears across the catalog: an entity that
partners with or inhabits a human host; a formation transmitted across generations that the inheritor did not
choose; a mechanism of beautification that makes the condition invisible to the person inside it; the failure of
sanctuary when the threat is already interior; and a cyclical structure that forecloses exit.
This paper asks why a similar pattern appears across albums with unrelated stated concepts, and proposes
that the pattern is recognisable because it resembles a real structural feature of the human condition that the
Qur’anic sources also describe. The Mars Volta did not derive their lyrics from the Qur’an. The convergence
is therefore evidence for the descriptive power of the proposed structure, not for the band’s theological
commitments.
1.2 The framework applied
Two prior papers by this author supply the interpretive scaffold.
The first, Inheritance, Transmission, and Turning Back (Arfeen, 2026a), develops a three-level architecture
of moral responsibility for a person formed inside a tradition he did not choose. Level one (inherited position)
carries no culpability: the pen does not record against a child, and corruption of the innate disposition (fitrah)
is framed as an injury done to him rather than an act he performed. Level two (sustained participation after
the arrival of capacity and a conclusive proof) is where responsibility attaches. Level three (transmission, the
act of forming the next generation) is where weight attaches and compounds with the number who follow.
The paper treats both responsible species (mankind and the concealed ones, jinn) together, examines their
collaboration as a worked case of joint corruption with undivided responsibility, and establishes that the
remedy (turning back, tawbah) is indexed to the person’s present position rather than to reconstruction of
his causal history.
The second, Computational Tawhid (Arfeen & Claude, 2026b), proposes that reality is a divine computation;
that consciousness is fundamental to all entities within it; that the human is a process endowed with genuine
agency, comprehensive discrimination (the Names), and a pre-installed alignment toward truth (fitra); and
that the adversarial architecture described in the Qur’an operates as corruption within the computation
rather than as a rival creative force.
1.3 What is claimed and what is not
Claimed: The Mars Volta’s lyrics independently articulate structural features that resemble those described
in the formed-agency architecture and the computational ontology, and the resemblance is specific enough to
be worth examining. The convergence is a Pillar 2 observation: it is suggestive rather than probative, and it
is subject to the convergence-illusion risk identified in the Computational Tawhid paper.
Not claimed: that Cedric Bixler-Zavala is encoding Qur’anic content, that the lyrics confirm the theological
framework, that the convergence establishes the framework’s truth, or that the lyrics are best read through
this lens rather than others. The paper presents one reading. It does not claim exclusivity.

2. Method and source handling
2.1 Corpus
The corpus comprises the seven studio albums released by The Mars Volta between 2003 and 2022: De-Loused
in the Comatorium (2003), Frances the Mute (2005), Amputechture (2006), The Bedlam in Goliath (2008),
Octahedron (2009), Noctourniquet (2012), and The Mars Volta (2022). The acoustic re-recording Que Dios Te
Maldiga Mi Corazon (2023) and the ninth studio album Lucro Sucio; Los Ojos del Vacio (2025) fall outside
the analytical window and are not coded.
2.2 Unit of analysis
The primary unit is the album. A motif is coded as present for an album if at least one song on the album
contains lyrical or structural features that correspond to the motif definition. A single song is sufficient for a
positive coding; absence requires that no song on the album contains such features. Ambiguous cases are
coded as ambiguous and noted.
2.3 Motif definitions
Five motifs were identified during the analysis. They emerged from the lyrical review rather than being
pre-specified, and the study is therefore exploratory rather than confirmatory. A future confirmatory test
would require pre-registration of motifs and independent coding.
M1: Entity–partnership. An entity that inhabits, accompanies, merges with, or operates from within a
human figure’s perceptual field. The entity is not merely external; it shapes the host’s experience from the
inside.
M2: Generational transmission. The condition the protagonist occupies was not chosen by him. It was
inherited, received, or transmitted from a prior generation or a pre-existing arrangement.
M3: Beautification. The formed person does not know he is formed. The condition is invisible to the
person inside it, and the perceptual or evaluative apparatus that would detect it has itself been shaped by
the condition.
M4: Failed sanctuary. A space or institution that should offer protection fails because the threat is already
inside it.
M5: Cyclical closure. The condition repeats. The structure forecloses exit not by force but by returning
the person to the starting point.
2.4 Source hierarchy
For artist statements: contemporary primary interviews and official liner material are preferred; later
recollections are secondary; secondary summaries (including Wikipedia-attributed statements) are tertiary
and flagged. For lyrics: official lyric sheets and licensed editions are preferred. Where unofficial transcriptions
are used, uncertainty is noted.
2.5 What would count as a failed mapping
The catalog-wide recurrence claim would fail if any album in the corpus showed no positive coding on a
majority of the five motifs, or if a comparator corpus (unrelated to occult, institutional, or religious themes)
showed equal motif density under the same definitions.

3.2 Album-by-album evidence
De-Loused in the Comatorium (2003). M1: the Comatorium itself is an interior architecture inhabited
by forces that shape the protagonist’s experience from within. M2: the protagonist Cerpin Taxt did not
choose his coma; his condition was precipitated by self-destruction modelled on a real person’s history (Julio
Venegas, who entered a coma after a deliberate overdose, recovered, and later took his own life by jumping
from a bridge; see Bixler-Zavala in contemporary 2003 interviews). M3: the Comatorium presents itself as
real while the waking world does not. M4: recovery from the coma (the ostensible sanctuary of waking life)
leads directly to suicide. M5: the protagonist exits one death-state and enters another.
Frances the Mute (2005). M1: the spectral figures (Miranda, Cassandra, the Widow) mediate the
protagonist’s search and are not merely external. M2: the search is for biological parents, tracing a lineage
the protagonist did not select. M3: the album’s own origin story (the diary found in a repossessed car) was
acknowledged as fabricated in the documentary Omar and Cedric: If This Ever Gets Weird (Rodriguez-Lopez
& Bixler-Zavala, 2017), which is itself a beautification: the audience was given a false account and treated it
as real. M4: “Cassandra Gemini” places death imagery (morgues, sarcophagi) inside the space of preservation.
M5: the album’s final passage returns to the opening guitar motif, structurally closing the loop.
Amputechture (2006). M1: the Baphomet and the forces invoked in “Day of the Baphomets.” M2:
“Meccamputechture” references “humans as ornaments” and inherited practices. M3: the Baphomet is
worshipped as genuine by those who cannot distinguish it from the real; the album’s overriding theme is the
abuse and fear of religion (PopMatters, 2006). M4: the religious institution itself is the source of the harm it
should protect against. M5: the cyclical character is structural rather than narrative (each self-contained
song circles its own theme).
The Bedlam in Goliath (2008). M1: Goliath, described by Cedric as a composite of one male and two
female voices channelled through the ouija board (Bixler-Zavala, Mojo, February 2008). M2: the board’s
history predates the band; the entities have their own prior existence. M3: “Wax Simulacra” (the copy
replacing the original) and “Ilyena” (“can’t spot through the lens”). M4: “Ilyena” (“bleeding through your
sanctuary”). M5: the album’s production narrative is cyclical (the board kept producing the same entities
night after night, and the band’s attempts to complete the album kept being disrupted). Cedric stated that
the album was “one huge metaphor for the way women are treated in Islamic society (honour killings, etc.)
not just a story about a ouija board” (Bixler-Zavala, in a statement preserved in Octahedron-era interview
reporting; Westword, January 2008).
Octahedron (2009). This is the album where the motif coding is most attenuated, and the honest assessment
is that it provides weaker evidence for the pattern than the other six. M1 (ambiguous): “Teflon” contains

imagery of hostage-taking and assassination, and “Halo of Nembutals” names a halo composed of sedatives
(an entity-like presence operating through chemical means), but neither presents a clearly interior entity
in the way the other albums do. M2 (positive): “Since We’ve Been Wrong” opens the album with an
acknowledgment of sustained error inherited across time. M3 (ambiguous): Cedric described “Luciforms”
as evoking the Salem Witch Trials and satirising the religious right’s treatment of women, which implies a

than on other albums. M4 (positive): “Teflon” (“what am I without the bruises? / this switch, it won’t come
on”) depicts a condition where the damage has become constitutive of identity, so that the self is its own
failed sanctuary. M5 (positive): the album’s restraint is itself cyclical – a return to quiet after the chaos of
Bedlam, replicating the coma-to-waking-to-death cycle of De-Loused.
Noctourniquet (2012). M1: the Hyacinthus figure and the Solomonic addresses. M2: the Solomon
Grundy lifecycle (born on Monday, died on Saturday) treats an entire human life as a deterministic loop the
protagonist did not author. M3: the title itself (nocturnal + tourniquet) describes something that stops the
bleeding at night – a concealment of the wound. M4: “held under the water in a blacklist trough / buried
in the plots of your front steps” locates the threat inside the domestic space. M5: the Solomon Grundy
structure is the purest cycle in the catalog – a nursery-rhyme loop that begins and ends in the same place.
The Mars Volta (2022). M1: the Church of Scientology as an institutional entity operating from within
Cedric’s family life. M2: Cedric’s Scientology involvement was introduced through his wife, who was
recruited through a social network; the self-titled album documents the multi-year process of extraction.
M3: “Blacklight Shine” reveals what is invisible under normal light; the lyrics address karma, culprits, and
concealed operations (Bixler-Zavala, singing in Spanish and English; see Beaumont-Thomas, 2022). M4: the
Church is a sanctuary (a religious community) that is the source of the harm. M5: the album’s existence (a
reunion after a decade-long split caused in part by Scientology) is itself evidence of cyclical return.
3.3 What the table shows and does not show
Six of seven albums show clear positive coding on all five motifs. Octahedron shows positive coding on three
motifs and ambiguous coding on two. The pattern is present across the catalog but is not uniformly strong.
The honest summary is that the structural recurrence is demonstrated across six albums with strong evidence
and across a seventh with partial evidence.
Two explanations remain live hypotheses, and the paper does not choose between them.
The first is artistic: Cedric has a set of thematic preoccupations that he projects onto whatever material he
is working with. This is ordinary for any artist with a consistent vision, and it does not require a theological
explanation. The seven albums are not independent observations; they share the same principal lyricist and
a long-running creative partnership, so cross-album recurrence is weaker evidence than recurrence across
genuinely independent artists or institutions.
The second is structural: the formed-agency architecture predicts that any sufficiently detailed artistic depiction
of institutional corruption will contain these features, because entity–partnership, inherited arrangement,
beautification, failed sanctuary, and cyclical closure are features of the phenomenon regardless of its local
instantiation. This prediction is testable in principle (by coding independent corpora against the same motif
definitions) but has not been tested in this study.

4. Case study: “Ilyena”
“Ilyena” is the third track on The Bedlam in Goliath (2008) and is selected as the primary case because it
condenses all five motifs into a single song and because Cedric’s stated intent for the song contradicts its
lyrical content in a way that is analytically productive.
4.1 The name
Cedric stated that he named the song after Helen Mirren’s birth name (“Ilyena”) and described the song
as “a little lighthearted and different from the subject matter,” part of his stated attempt to counter the

5

male spirit by invoking figures such as Metatron, Ariel, and St. Gabriel (Bixler-Zavala, Marquee magazine,
January 2008). Within the album’s narrative, the name is also associated with one of the voices channelled
through the ouija board.
The name therefore operates on two registers: a protective naming strategy (invoking a real, powerful,
surviving woman) and the name of a figure within the demonological narrative. Under the formed-agency
architecture, this duality is structurally resonant: the same word can function as shield and wound because
the partnership itself is a condition in which protection and threat share a single locus.
4.2 Structural mapping
The exhausted ritual cycle (M5). The song opens with chants that have cycled. Under the formed-agency
architecture, the cycling is the generational transmission reaching completion: the protocol has run its loop.
“How can I go wrong” reads as the deterministic confidence of a completed process.
The Edenic inversion (M2). “There will be no Eve for Adam / If the apples have gone.” Under the
architecture’s treatment of the innate disposition (fitrah), corruption of the default is an injury inflicted, not
a choice exercised. The apples being “gone” is not a transgression but an absence – a completed operation in
which the Edenic possibility has been foreclosed.
Incarnated debts and incarceration (M2, M1). “I need a brand new skin / Incarnated debts /
Incarcerated when the deeds are left to purchase.” Under the architecture, “incarnated debts” maps onto
level one: obligations that have taken flesh, arriving before the child could consent. “Incarcerated when the
deeds are left to purchase” describes being imprisoned before the transaction is complete. The song does not
resolve whether the speaker is the entity seeking a new host or the child describing his own condition. In the
cohabitation model the architecture describes, the two voices are indistinguishable from inside.
The chain of contamination (M1, M3). “Entity ingredients / A mass of blackened scapes begins.”
“Entity ingredients” treats the adversarial agent as assembled from components rather than naturally born,
which is consistent with the Computational Tawhid framework’s treatment of the adversarial process as
corruption within the computation. “Scapes” evokes the scapegoat mechanism, and the “mass” is both a
quantity and a ceremony.
The invitation into death. “Follow me to die / Follow me into oblivion.” Under the architecture, this
maps onto 14:22: the tempter’s authority extends to calling, not compelling. “Follow me” is an invitation.
The song does not resolve whether the person follows freely or under duress, and the architecture holds that
both conditions are real and differently weighted.
Concealment and failed sanctuary (M3, M4). “Can’t spot through the lens / Intention accident /
Bleeding through your sanctuary.” Under the architecture, the lens that cannot detect the condition has
been shaped by the formation (43:36–37: the companion bars from the path while the person supposes
himself guided). “Intention accident” compresses the structure of institutional concealment: intentional harm
classified as accidental. “Bleeding through your sanctuary” locates the violence inside the space of protection.
4.3 The register mismatch as evidence
Cedric described “Ilyena” as “lighthearted” while its lyrics describe the dissolution of identity within an
inescapable ritual loop. The NME review noted the song was “creepy and unsettling” despite veering “close
to electropop danceability” (NME, 1 February 2008). The musical surface presents safety while the words
describe annihilation. Under the Computational Tawhid framework, this register mismatch is itself consistent
with the beautification mechanism: the arrangement presents itself as pleasant while operating as something
else.

5. The spectrum of participation
The analysis reveals a spectrum of participation rather than a single category of agent. This spectrum is the
interpretive feature that distinguishes the replacement framework from the withdrawn uniform-conspiracy
model recorded in Appendix B of the transmission paper.
6

5.1 Equal partners
In “Day of the Baphomets” (Amputechture), agents are depicted as active collaborators: offering entrails,
born to bring death, planting vermin deliberately. Under the architecture, these are agents at level three.
5.2 The coerced
In “Ilyena,” the entity’s request to “hide with me identity” and the bleeding through sanctuary describe a
condition in which resistance meets retaliation from within the person’s own perceptual field. Under the
architecture, 16:106 covers this case.
5.3 The unknowing
In “Wax Simulacra,” the copy replaces the original and the person cannot tell the difference. In the selftitled album, Cedric’s years inside Scientology are described in retrospect as genuine delusion. Under the
architecture, this maps onto 43:37: those barred from the path while supposing themselves guided.
5.4 What the lyrics do not contain
The lyrics do not contain a remedy. Across seven albums and two decades, the catalog documents the
condition of being formed inside a corrupt arrangement and offers no instruction for what to do about it.
The architecture supplies what the lyrics lack: turning back (tawbah), indexed to present position, available
because the innate disposition persists beneath the formation at a level the formation cannot reach (30:30).
Whether this absence in the lyrics is diagnostic (evidence that observation alone cannot provide the exit) or
merely reflects artistic choice, the paper cannot determine. The observation is noted; the inference is not
drawn.

6. The self-correction
6.1 What happened during the analysis
The initial interpretive exercise applied a framework in which most of the human race is covertly partnered
with adversarial entities. The withdrawn document (recorded in Arfeen 2026a, Appendix B) is described
there as positing a multi-generational deception involving lifelong induced states from infancy, a fabricated
history, a divinely protected minority persecuted by a deluded majority, and validation through persecution.
The framework mapped onto the Mars Volta lyrics with apparent precision.
The framework was then recognised as the same one withdrawn in Appendix B, for two reasons: it contradicted
itself (involuntary subjugation from infancy is incompatible with divine justice), and it had no remedy.
The replacement architecture, applied to the same lyrics, produced a different reading. The lyrics describe a
spectrum of participation rather than a uniform conspiracy. The inheritor’s defense is sincere rather than
performed. The beautification makes the person suppose himself guided, which is a different claim from
conscious performance. And the remedy is available because the fitrah persists beneath the formation.
6.2 Why the self-correction matters
The episode demonstrates that the retracted framework can generate a coherent and initially persuasive
interpretation of this corpus. Its retraction is not because it fails to describe the observations but because it
fails structurally: it contradicts its own premises and offers no remedy. The replacement architecture describes
the same observations without those structural failures. One important test of a superseding framework is
whether it can preserve the predecessor’s explanatory successes while removing its structural defects.
The strongest reading of this episode is methodological: the same corpus can sustain a superficially precise
but structurally defective reading, so apparent precision of fit is not sufficient. That lesson directly reinforces
the convergence-illusion warning.

7. Confounds and limitations
7.1 The convergence-illusion risk
The Computational Tawhid paper identifies this as the primary vulnerability of any framework that finds
structural convergence across independent domains. The analyst’s prior commitment to the Qur’anic
framework shapes which lyrical features are selected, how they are interpreted, and which mappings are
deemed significant. Complete objectivity is not claimed.
7.2 Source independence is not analytical independence
The Mars Volta did not have access to the author’s theological framework. The source corpus is therefore
independent of the framework’s production. However, the analyst did have access to the framework, and
the mapping is not an independent test. Source independence protects against deliberate encoding or direct
influence. It does not protect against analyst-side confirmation bias. A genuinely independent test would
require pre-registered motif definitions and blind coding by independent raters.
7.3 Corpus dependence
The seven albums share the same principal lyricist and a long-running creative partnership. Cross-album
recurrence within one artist’s corpus is weaker evidence than recurrence across genuinely independent artists
or institutions. A future stronger test would pre-specify the five motifs and score corpora produced by
independent creators.
7.4 Feature dependence
The five motifs are not necessarily independent. Narratives about cults, institutions, abuse, inheritance,
or corrupt systems naturally tend to co-produce internalised control, concealment, failed protection, and
recurrence. A five-motif match may therefore contain fewer than five independent pieces of information.
7.5 Motif-selection flexibility
The five motifs emerged during the analysis rather than being pre-specified. The study is therefore exploratory.
Metaphorical lyrics can often be coded into broad motif definitions in multiple ways, and a post-hoc motif set
cannot distinguish genuine structural convergence from flexible pattern-matching. The paper labels the study
accordingly.
7.6 No comparator framework or negative control
The paper acknowledges that Jungian, Marxist, or Gnostic readings might also produce plausible mappings.
Neither an alternative framework nor a negative-control corpus (one not selected for occult, institutional, or
religious themes) has been tested. The absence of a comparator limits the specificity claim.
7.7 The fabrication of origin stories
Cedric and Omar acknowledged in a documentary that the backstory for Frances the Mute was fabricated
(Rodriguez-Lopez & Bixler-Zavala, 2017). Retrospective artist explanations may differ across time and
promotional contexts. The source hierarchy in Section 2.4 addresses this, but the reader should note that
artist-supplied narratives are not treated as ground truth.
7.8 Lyric-source provenance
The analysis turns on specific phrases. Where official lyric sheets were unavailable, unofficial transcriptions
were used with the understanding that transcription errors are possible. Uncertainty is noted in the case
study where exact wording matters.

8. Conclusion
Three levels of claim should be distinguished.
Observed within this analysis. Five structural motifs (entity–partnership, generational transmission,
beautification, failed sanctuary, cyclical closure) can be mapped across six of seven albums in the Mars Volta
corpus with clear positive coding, and across the seventh with partial evidence. The motifs emerged during
the analysis and were not pre-specified.
Interpretive claim. These motifs resemble features of the formed-agency architecture developed in the
companion papers: the three-level structure of moral responsibility, the partnership between mankind and the
concealed ones, the beautification mechanism at 43:36–37, the failed sanctuary when the threat is internal, and
the cyclical closure of generational transmission. The resemblance is specific enough to be worth examining
and is subject to the confounds identified in Section 7.
Framework claim. The formed-agency architecture supplies a remedy (turning back, tawbah) and an
ontological ground (the persistence of the innate disposition beneath the formation, 30:30) that the lyrics do
not contain. This is a claim of the applied architecture, not a result established by the Mars Volta analysis.
The convergence between the lyrical corpus and the theological framework is suggestive. It is not probative.
The analysis is exploratory, the confounds are real, and a confirmatory test would require pre-registered motifs,
independent coding, and a comparator corpus. What the analysis demonstrates is that the structural features
described in the formed-agency architecture are independently recognisable in a corpus produced without
access to the framework. Whether that recognisability reflects shared structural origin, common features
of institutional-corruption narratives, or the analyst’s pattern-detection apparatus imposing coherence on
independent artistic decisions is a question the analysis raises and does not resolve.
