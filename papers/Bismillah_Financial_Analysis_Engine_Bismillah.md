LAYER 2: Wave and Pattern Structure
Elliott Wave Theory (Frost and Prechter) Markets move in 5-wave impulses followed by 3-wave
corrections.
Three inviolable rules:
1. Wave 2 NEVER retraces beyond Wave 1's origin
2. Wave 4 NEVER overlaps Wave 1's price territory
3. Wave 3 is NEVER the shortest
Key Fibonacci relationships:
• W2 = 0.618 × W1 (or 0.5 × W1)
• W3 = 1.618 × W1 (or 2.618 × W1)
• W4 = 0.382 × W3 (or 0.5 × W3)
• W5 = W1 or 0.618 × (W1 through W3 net distance)
• Alternation guideline: sharp W2 → sideways W4 and vice versa
Corrective patterns: zigzag (5-3-5), flat (3-3-5), triangle (3-3-3-3-3), combinations.

LAYER 3: Volatility and Roughness
Hurst Exponent (H) The single most important diagnostic for any time series:
• H < 0.5: Mean-reverting (anti-persistent). Pullbacks tend to reverse. Buy dips, sell rips.
• H = 0.5: Random walk. No edge from directional betting.
• H > 0.5: Trending (persistent). Momentum strategies work. Let winners run.
Operational rule: Calculate H on the specific asset and timeframe being analyzed. Do not assume. H
changes over time (regime change), and the direction of change is itself informative. Declining H =
trending market becoming random = reduce directional exposure. Rising H = random market becoming
trending = increase directional exposure.
Measured reference values:
• BTC full history: H = 0.631 (trending)
• BTC 2023-2026: H = 0.554 (declining toward random — regime change signal)
• Defense stocks (typical): H = 0.43-0.55 (mixed, slightly mean-reverting near-term)
Volatility is Rough (Gatheral et al.) Log-volatility across all asset classes behaves as fractional
Brownian motion with H ≈ 0.1 — much rougher than standard BM. This means volatility clusters are
spikier and more concentrated than standard models predict. Implication: standard VaR and options
pricing systematically underestimate tail risk.
HAR-RV (Corsi 2009) Heterogeneous Autoregressive Realized Volatility. Multi-scale cascade: daily
(β=0.36), weekly (β=0.28), monthly (β=0.28) components. Volatility at each scale feeds into the next,
creating nested oscillations.

LAYER 5: Position Sizing and Risk
Kelly Criterion Optimal bet size = (bp - q) / b, where b = odds, p = probability of win, q = probability
of loss.
CRITICAL: Always use Fractional Kelly (1/4 to 1/2 of full Kelly). Full Kelly maximizes long-term
growth rate but produces severe drawdowns. Half-Kelly achieves 75% of the growth rate with
dramatically lower drawdowns. Quarter-Kelly is appropriate when probability estimates are uncertain
(which they always are).
Practical application for small accounts ($200-$5,000):
• Never risk more than 2% of total capital on a single trade
• With $200 capital, maximum risk per trade = $4
• This means stops must be tight OR position sizes must be minimal
• At this capital level, the primary value is learning the framework through live execution, not
generating wealth

11. GÖDELIAN FRAMEWORK NOTE

describe arithmetic contains true statements unprovable from within. The financial application: no
analytical framework, no matter how comprehensive, can fully model the system it operates within.

appropriate response is epistemic humility: present findings as probability assessments, maintain
multiple scenarios, and never claim certainty.
