2. WHAT IS THE GOLDEN RATIO?
The golden ratio is the only number in mathematics with this property:

If you add 1 to it, you get its square. If you subtract 1 from its inverse, you get the same
inverse.
phi = 1.6180339887
phi + 1 = 2.6180339887 = phi2
1 / phi = 0.6180339887 = phi - 1
Put another way: phi is the number where the relationship between the whole and the larger part is the
same as the relationship between the larger part and the smaller part. This is called self-similarity — the
same proportion repeating at every scale.
Visually, when you draw a rectangle with sides in the ratio 1:1.618 and then cut a square from it, the
remaining rectangle has the exact same proportions as the original. You can keep cutting squares forever
and get the same ratio. If you connect the corners of these nested rectangles with a smooth curve, you get
the golden spiral.
This spiral appears — measurably, verifiably — at every scale in the physical world.
