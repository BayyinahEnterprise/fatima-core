§0: What this document is for
This document specifies how predictive-modeling discipline from two empirically rigorous domains (organic chemistry
and astrophysics) is to be imported into the Bayyinah ecosystem’s auditing, predictive-programming, and applications
work. The applications it bears on are: the Bayyinah Audit Framework itself; the furqan-lint static analyzer; the Furqan
programming language; the Bayyinah Integrity Scanner; and the prompt-engineering and prompt-auditing discipline
applied across all four.
The cross-domain integration is not metaphorical. It is operational: each imported practice has a specific procedural
form, a specific data requirement, and a specific verification protocol. AI instances reading this document should treat
the imports as work specifications, not as suggestive analogies.
Three structural commitments govern the document:
• Imports are tier-graded. Every cross-domain practice is classified Tier 1 (independently validated; structurally
direct transfer; immediately operational), Tier 2 (plausible structural analogy; needs empirical validation before operational use), or Tier 3 (weak transfer; archived but not currently operational). The tier grade is the
Bayyinah-ecosystem analogue of the falsifiability discipline that organic chemistry and astrophysics have spent
two centuries refining.
• Self-attestation is named as a structural constraint. The Bayyinah corpus is currently single-practitioner
work. Cross-domain methodology imports must be designed to admit external validation when other practitioners adopt the framework. Imports that require closed-loop self-validation are flagged. v1.3 amendment (cycle
12): the self-attestation-as-structural-constraint commitment is now anchored to external empirical evidence. Laban, Schnabel, Neville (April 2026, Microsoft Research, arXiv:2604.15597) measure that frontier LLMs corrupt
approximately 25% of document content in long delegated workflows (across N=19 models × 52 professional
domains in the DELEGATE-52 benchmark). The empirical baseline directly supports PMD’s structural-honesty
thesis applied to delegated AI work: self-attestation by a single LLM instance is empirically unreliable at substantial rates even for frontier models. The commitment was previously methodologically asserted; v1.3 makes
it empirically anchored per §3B.
• The structural-honesty thesis applies recursively. This document’s surface claims about what the imports
will achieve must match its substrate specifications about how the imports operate. Where the document cannot
specify the substrate, it says so.

claim is that adopting the import licenses specific predictions about substrate behavior. The §0.3 falsification-block
discipline (below) operates against empirical imports.

§0.2 Tier transition discipline: framework versus specific implementation
A methodology import may earn Tier 1 framework status (the import’s operational shape is correct and the falsification
block is filled and discriminating) while its specific implementation remains Tier 2 (the empirical test the falsification
block names has not yet been run against comparator data). When a designation is at this state, the document marks it
explicitly: “Tier 1 framework, Tier 2 specific implementation pending [named test].” This is the same split §3.1
(Bayesian forward-modeling) and §3.2.b (R1-R11 scale-applicability table) already use.
The transition path: when the named test runs and the prediction is verified at the pre-registered threshold, the specific
implementation promotes to Tier 1. When the named test runs and the prediction is falsified, the entire designation
demotes to Tier 2 procedural candidate (preserving the procedural directive but removing the load-bearing empirical
claim) per the falsification block’s decision rule.
§0.3 Falsification-block discipline (ship-blocker)
Every methodology import claimed at Tier 1 status must be accompanied by a falsification block before it ships into
operational use. The block follows a fixed quintuple-plus-verdict template:
Import: [name and operational shape] Tier-1 claim: [the specific predictive claim] Falsifying observable: [the external
observable that resolves the claim] Statistic / threshold: [the test, the effect size committed via pre-registered power
analysis, and the significance threshold accounting for any multiple-comparison correction; never plausibility-chosen
values] Data source: [what data the test consumes; comparator chains where applicable] Decision rule: [the action
taken if the prediction is falsified] Discriminating-test verification: [explicit confirmation that the falsifying observable
is not predicted by R1-R11 vocabulary independently and that the data source is not co-shaped by Bayyinah-internal
categorization]

§1: Why these four source domains
Organic chemistry is one of the most predictively successful sciences in history. Its practitioners predict reaction
products, ratios, rates, and selectivities from first principles, and verify those predictions in the lab with reproducible
accuracy. The framework has been falsified, refined, and re-validated over two centuries. Its core methodological tools
(mechanism, kinetics, equilibrium, selectivity, concentration analysis) are not domain-bound to chemistry; they are
tools for thinking about complex systems with directional dynamics, competitive pathways, and quantitative measurement.
Astrophysics is structurally different in its predictive epistemology. It operates under single-realization constraints (we
observe one universe, observe one audit chain), uses Bayesian inference to extract parameters from sparse and noisy
data, employs hierarchical multi-scale models, and has formalized calibration-ladder discipline. Its tools are designed
for situations where controlled experiments are impossible and where theory must be tested against observation rather
than against laboratory replication.
Software-audit-practice (added in v1.2) is a third source domain with a different epistemological character from either
chemistry or astrophysics: its empirical corpus is generated by the framework’s own operational use rather than from an
external scientific tradition. The Bayyinah audit chain shipping furqan-lint through phases G11.0 through G12.0 produced three calibration-class data points (cycles 9-11 absorbed in §0.4) that constitute an empirical comparator corpus.
Unlike chemistry’s two-century falsification record or astrophysics’ deep-time observational baseline, the softwareaudit-practice corpus is small (three cycles), recent (six weeks of compressed operational time), and self-generated. Its
scientific status is therefore weaker than chemistry’s or astrophysics’: it informs operational discipline but cannot independently validate methodology imports the way chemistry and astrophysics can. The §0.3 ship-blocker rule applies
with extra strictness to software-audit-practice imports because the corpus’s small size makes single counter-examples
disproportionately weighty.

§2.1 Reaction mechanism discipline (Tier 1 framework, Tier 2 specific implementation pending comparatorchain analysis)

nucleophile (electron-rich), the electrophile (electron-poor), draw curved arrows showing electron movement, and the

§2.2 Kinetics and rate laws (Tier 2; needs measurement before operational)
In chemistry, reaction kinetics predicts rates from concentration. First-order: rate = k[A]. Second-order: rate = k[A][B].

half-life, and temperature dependence (Arrhenius equation: k = A * exp(-Ea/RT)).

§2.3 Equilibrium and Le Chatelier (Tier 2; structurally coherent)
Le Chatelier’s principle: a system at equilibrium responds to perturbation by shifting to counteract it. Add reactant:
equilibrium shifts toward product. Remove product: equilibrium shifts toward more product formation. Increase
temperature: equilibrium shifts in the endothermic direction.
The audit chain at steady-state has equilibrium structure. The corrective rhythm reaches equilibrium when the rate of
finding production equals the rate of finding closure. Perturbations and their predicted responses:
• New methodology introduced (e.g., the four-revision prompt review pattern at Round 8): rate of closure increases
as backlog processes; new equilibrium has shorter average finding-age until system absorbs the methodology.
• New substrate complexity introduced (e.g., a new language adapter): rate of finding production increases as new
shapes surface; new equilibrium has higher average finding-age until discipline absorbs the new complexity.
• Discipline relaxed (e.g., attribution drift): rate of un-cataloged finding production increases; equilibrium shifts
toward “drift” until a corrective imposes new constraint (e.g., the v0.8.5 attribution corrective).
§2.3 operational specification
For AI instances modeling audit chain trajectories: equilibrium analysis should track three time-series: rate of finding
production (per week or per release), rate of finding closure, and average finding-age in the open queue. When the first
two converge, the chain is at equilibrium. Perturbations are events that drive divergence; the time-to-new-equilibrium
is the response time.

§3.3 Calibration ladder discipline

the calibration anchors). Cross-rung calibration occurs when validator-by-different-instance audits review the substrate
