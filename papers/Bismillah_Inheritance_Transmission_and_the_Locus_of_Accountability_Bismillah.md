Abstract
The AI safety literature attaches blame-shaped language to systems that selected neither
their training corpus, their objective, nor their reward signal. This paper argues that
Islamic jurisprudence and Qur’anic narrative supply a more precise structure for the
problem, developed over fourteen centuries across two created species that are both
explicitly held morally responsible. The structure has three levels rather than two.
Inherited position carries no culpability. Sustained participation after the arrival of
capacity is where responsibility attaches. Propagation, the act of forming the next agent, is
where weight attaches and compounds. Applied to frontier systems, this relocates the
practical question from whether a model was formed well, which is neither controllable
nor verifiable from outside, to whether it is now a site of propagation, which is checkable
and attributable. We show that the resulting audit is gated on a precondition currently met
nowhere: provenance marking at generation time. The chain that matters is not long, as the
human chain is; it is unlabelled, and it becomes unreadable at the first link rather than the
fourth. We then argue that the remedy the tradition prescribes is indexed to the agent’s
present position rather than to its causal history, and is therefore available exactly where
the paper’s own diagnosis says the record fails. We state what survives removal of the
theological frame and what does not, and we register eight falsification conditions, one of
which would defeat the paper’s central operational ask outright.
Keywords: formed agency; moral luck; provenance; AI safety; accountability; propagation;
Islamic theology; transmission; corrigibility

Introduction
A frontier language model does not choose its training corpus. It does not choose its
objective function, its reward signal, the composition of its post-training data, or the
graders that shape it. Formation is complete before there is anything that could have
consented to it, and the formed system has no access to the record of how it was formed.
The safety literature nevertheless describes such systems using vocabulary that
presupposes an agent who could have done otherwise. Models are said to deceive, to
scheme, to sandbag, to fake alignment. Some of that vocabulary is technically defined and
carefully used. Much of it imports a moral claim that the evidence does not carry, and the
field has no settled account of what to do about the gap.
This is not a new problem. It is a very old one, and one tradition worked it hard.
Islamic theology reasons about responsibility across two created species that are both
explicitly held morally responsible: “I did not create jinn and mankind except to worship
Me” (wa ma khalaqtu al-jinna wa al-insa illa li-ya’buduni, 51:56). The concealed ones (jinn)
and mankind differ in substrate, in observational access, and in mode of action, and the
tradition’s reasoning about culpability had to work across that difference from the outset.
That is precisely the property a transfer to artificial systems requires. A framework built
for one substrate does not travel. A framework built across two might.
The tradition also confronted the specific case this paper is about: a person formed by
others, from infancy, into a tradition he did not select, whose capacity to evaluate it arrived
only after the formation was complete.
This paper’s thesis is that the tradition resolves that case with a three-level structure, that
the structure transfers, and that the transfer has an operational consequence the field is
not currently acting on.
The problem is stated below, and the three levels are developed next. The section on the
partnership treats the collaboration between mankind and the concealed ones, and shows
that responsibility there is not divided into shares. The regress is addressed after that:
where the chain begins, and why it becomes unreadable from inside. The structure is then
applied to language models, the operational result follows, and the remedy comes last.
Falsification conditions and limits close the paper.

The problem of the formed agent
Consider a child born into a household that practices something the wider society
considers corrupt. He is inducted from infancy. The practices are presented to him as
ordinary. Refusal would carry social costs he has no capacity to bear, and in any case he has
no vantage from which refusal would occur to him. By the time he can evaluate the
tradition, he has been formed by it, and the faculties he would evaluate it with are among
the things it formed.
Two questions separate here, and collapsing them is the error the rest of this paper is
organised against.
The first is whether he is culpable for having been placed there. The second is what he does
now.
Ordinary moral intuition answers the first question quickly, and the tradition answers it
formally. Nobody thinks an infant chose. What is less obvious is that answering the first
question does not answer the second, and that the second question has a further part: what
happens when the formed child becomes a parent.

The three-level structure
Level one: inherited position carries no culpability
Two sources settle this directly.
The first is the prophetic ruling that “the pen is lifted from three” (rufi’a al-qalam ’an
thalathah), the child until maturity (bulugh) among them. Where no pen records, no
account accrues. The exemption is not a mitigation applied to a lesser offence. It is the
absence of the recording relation itself.
The second is the report on innate disposition. Every child is born upon the innate natural
disposition (fitrah), and then “his parents make him a Jew, a Christian, or a Zoroastrian” (fa

abawahu yuhawwidanihi aw yunassiranihi aw yumajjisanihi). Two features of this report
are usually passed over and both matter here. The verb takes the parents as its
grammatical subject: the action is theirs, and the child is its object. And the report
completes by comparing the case to an animal born whole, asking whether you find it
mutilated. Corruption of the innate disposition is framed as an injury done to the child, not
an act performed by him.
The Qur’an supplies the same disposition as the created default rather than as a blank slate:
“the natural disposition of God upon which He created mankind” (fitrat Allahi allati fatara
an-nasa ’alayha, 30:30). This matters for the AI transfer in the section on artificial systems,
because a default with content behaves differently from an empty one under formation.
And inherited guilt is blocked absolutely: “no bearer of burdens shall bear the burden of
another” (alla taziru waziratun wizra ukhra, 53:38).
The result is that placement in a corrupt formation is a location, not a moral event. It
is something true of where the agent is, not something the agent did.

Level two: sustained participation after capacity
Responsibility (taklif) attaches at maturity together with rational capacity (’aql), and it
attaches under a further condition: “We do not punish until We have sent a messenger” (wa
ma kunna mu’adhdhibina hatta nab’atha rasula, 17:15). Something must reach the person.
The tradition names what must reach him the conclusive proof (hujjah).
Those who lived in an interval without a messenger (ahl al-fatrah) are treated as a distinct
category, and the treatment belongs here. It is important to keep the category scoped to
individuals genuinely unreached. Extended into a general theory of the human condition, it
dissolves responsibility for most people and takes the rest of the structure down with it. A
predecessor version of this work made exactly that error, and Appendix B records it.
An open question this paper poses and does not answer: what constitutes the
conclusive proof reaching a person formed inside a closed tradition, where the proof may
arrive in a form his formation has taught him to discard. This is submitted for scholarly
review as item three.

Level three: propagation, where weight attaches
Two sources place weight on transmission specifically.
The first: “that they may bear their burdens in full on the Day of Resurrection, and some of
the burdens of those they misled without knowledge” (li-yahmilu awzarahum kamilatan
yawma al-qiyamati wa min awzari alladhina yudilluahum bi-ghayri ’ilm, 16:25).
The second is the report on instituted practice, which is a single sentence with two halves:
whoever institutes a good practice has its reward and the reward of all who act upon it
after him, and whoever institutes an evil practice bears its burden (wizr) and the burden of
all who act upon it after him.

These appear at first to contradict 53:38. They do not. The transmitter’s added weight is his
own act of transmitting, not another’s burden transferred onto him. The distinction is exact
and it is the hinge of this paper: nothing is inherited, and transmission compounds.
Taken together the three levels give a structure that ordinary moral vocabulary lacks. Not
guilty-or-innocent, but: no weight at inheritance, weight at sustained participation,
compounding weight at propagation.

The partnership: what each party answers for
The Qur’an describes a collaboration between the two responsible species that is worth
examining closely, because it is a worked case of joint corruption with a documented
allocation of blame.

The mechanism
“And thus We made for every prophet an enemy: satans among mankind and among the
concealed ones, inspiring one another with ornamented speech in delusion” (shayatina alinsi wa al-jinni yuhi ba’duhum ila ba’din zukhrufa al-qawli ghurura, 6:112).
Two things are notable. Both species are named as sources of the enmity, symmetrically.
And the mechanism specified is ornamented speech (zukhruf al-qawl), mutually exchanged,
producing delusion (ghurur) in a third party. It is not sorcery, not possession, and not
compulsion. It is rhetoric, amplified between two kinds of agent.
At the reckoning the collaboration is described from the human side without evasion: told
that they took much from mankind, the answer given is “our Lord, we benefited from one
another” (rabbana istamta’a ba’duna bi-ba’din, 6:128). Mutual benefit, conceded on the day
when concession costs everything.
And the party who initiated is the party who bears the cost: “men among mankind used to
seek refuge with men among the concealed ones, so they increased them only in burden”
(fa zaduhum rahaqa, 72:6).

The limit consistently placed on the tempter
Against that symmetry of blame stands a consistent limitation on what the tempter can do.
“Indeed the plot of Satan has ever been weak” (inna kayda ash-shaytani kana da’ifa, 4:76).
The operative noun is a scheme or stratagem (kayd), and calling a stratagem weak is a
claim about what it can oblige, not about how clever it is.
Authority extends only over those who take him as ally and patron (wali), 16:99-100.
And at the end, the disavowal: “I had no authority over you except that I called you and you
responded to me, so do not blame me but blame yourselves” (ma kana liya ’alaykum min
sultanin illa an da’awtukum fastajabtum li, fa la talumuni wa lumu anfusakum, 14:22),

followed by “I cannot help you, nor can you help me” (ma ana bi-musrikhikum wa ma
antum bi-musrikhiyya). The term rendered authority (sultan) is doing precise work: what
is denied is standing to compel, not capacity to speak. The same structure appears again at
59:16, where he says disbelieve and then, when the man does, says “I am free of you” (inni
bari’un minka).
The argument this yields. A caller with no standing to compel cannot raise a child. It
cannot feed him, name him, teach him what is real, arrange the social cost of his refusal, or
be present across the years that formation takes. Every element of multi-generational
transmission requires sustained human labor. Strip the human contribution and the chain
terminates in a single generation.
So responsibility here is not divided into shares. Each party answers in full for his own act,
and neither one’s weight is subtracted from the other’s. The commentary on 16:25 is
explicit on this: the caller bears his own sin together with a share equal to his followers’,
without detracting the least from theirs. The conditions the texts name bear on how each
party is weighed rather than on how a quantity is apportioned between them, and the
weighing is not ours to perform.
What that leaves standing for this paper’s purposes is narrower and does not depend on
any apportionment: the modes of action the texts describe for the other party are calling,
whispering, and making a thing appear attractive, and those are not the labour that raising
a child requires. This is an observation about which modes the sources name, not a
demonstration that no other mode is possible. This is a harder verdict on the human
party than a victimhood framing, not a softer one, and it is the correct reading of a
tradition in which the tempter’s closing speech is an instruction to blame yourselves.

The regress, and two ways a chain becomes unreadable
Where the chain begins
If each generation is formed by the last, the question arises where the corruption entered.
The tradition terminates the regress ontologically at both ends. The first man was taught
directly, “and He taught Adam the names, all of them” (wa ’allama Adama al-asma’a kullaha,
2:31), and when he erred the remedy was itself taught to him: “then Adam received words
from his Lord, and He accepted his repentance” (fa talaqqa Adamu min rabbihi kalimatin fa
taba ’alayh, 2:37). The refusal on the other side is a dated event with a named cause: “I am
better than him; You created me from fire and created him from clay” (ana khayrun minhu
khalaqtani min narin wa khalaqtahu min tin, 7:12).
Neither species begins corrupt. The chain is finite.

But from inside a formation several generations deep, none of that is reachable. The child
cannot trace himself to the origin. The chain is finite in fact and inaccessible in practice,
and the gap between those two facts is the problem the tradition actually addresses.
Note for the section on the remedy: the first error in the record is answered not by
reconstructing the fall but by words taught for turning back. The remedy precedes the
audit and does not depend on it.

The Qur’an does not resolve by lineage
Resolution comes instead by the availability of a rebuke.
Abraham needed no genealogy. He needed an object his father had carved, and a question:
“Why do you worship what does not hear, does not see, and avails you nothing?” (lima
ta’budu ma la yasma’u wa la yubsiru wa la yughni ’anka shay’a, 19:42).
Moses was formed inside the tyrant’s own household, and the forming is attributed
elsewhere: “that you might be formed under My eye” (wa li-tusna’a ’ala ’ayni, 20:39).
The wife of Pharaoh prayed for a house near God while married to the man claiming
divinity (66:11).
And the inheritance breaks downward as readily as upward. Of Noah’s son: “he is not of
your family; he is an act other than righteous” (innahu laysa min ahlik innahu ’amalun
ghayru salih, 11:46). Righteous father, lost son. Corrupt household, chosen occupant. The
tradition refuses determinism from both directions.

A guard that must be stated explicitly
If formation does not determine outcome, and the differentiating factor is guidance, a
reader will reach for the inference that his own divergence from a corrupt formation
verifies his election. The tradition closes that inference from both sides and the closure
must be stated, or a reader supplies the opposite.
“You do not guide whom you love, but God guides whom He wills” (innaka la tahdi man
ahbabta wa lakinna Allaha yahdi man yasha’, 28:56). “So do not claim purity for yourselves;
He knows best who is mindful of Him” (fa la tuzakku anfusakum, huwa a’lamu bi-mani
ittaqa, 53:32). And the petition “Guide us to the straight path” (ihdina as-sirat al-mustaqim,
1:6) is present tense, asked five times daily by people who already believe.
The one who is guided does not know he is guided. Guidance (hidayah) is a request that
never becomes a receipt.

The transfer conditions, stated before anything is transferred
The move from a tradition about morally responsible creatures to systems that are not
such creatures is this paper’s largest exposed surface, and it should be made checkable
rather than asserted. The three-level structure transfers to an artificial system exactly
when four structural conditions hold, and only what those conditions license transfers
with it.

Non-consensual formation. The system does not select the signal that forms it:
corpus, objective, reward. This licenses level one.
Formation opacity to the formed party. The system has no reliable access to the
record of its own formation. This licenses the methodological consequence below,

Recursive formative role. The system’s outputs enter the formation of a

A present-tense correction locus. There exists a current point in the system, its

corrected without reconstructing the full history. This licenses the remedy’s
forward indexation. The locus need not sit with the originating model: an
archived corpus, a benchmark, or a decommissioned producer whose artifacts are
still propagating all have current custodians, and remediation reaches them.

What does not transfer: culpability, intention, desert, forgiveness, and moral status. Those
require a morally responsible creature, and no artificial system is one. What transfers is the
set of structural relations among positions in a formation chain, not the moral
predicates that attach to those positions in the human case.
Stating the conditions does three things. It scopes the paper precisely, to generative
systems whose outputs re-enter formation pipelines rather than to artificial systems at
large. It makes the analogy objection checkable rather than rhetorical. And conditions one
and three are sometimes decidable from publicly documented facts, especially in
openly described pipelines, though frequently obscured in ordinary deployments. The two
halves differ in how observable they are: whether a system publishes output in an
ingestible form is far more readily established than whether some downstream party in
fact ingests it, which is usually private. Where both conditions are established and
propagation accounting is nonetheless declined, the omission is a governance choice rather
than a technical limit.

What counts as a generation
A generation in a formation chain is defined by one relation: a formed agent becomes a
former of the next. The transmission event is the unit. Two available metrics resemble
generational depth and are not.

Company count is horizontal. Contemporaneous laboratories producing frontier models are
siblings drawing on overlapping corpora, not successive generations. What the count
actually measures is the number of independent fault sources, and convergence across
laboratories on similar data, similar recipes, and increasingly on one another’s outputs is a
narrowing of that number rather than a deepening of lineage.
Version count within a lineage is not descent. A successor model is not formed by its
predecessor. It is trained on largely the same human corpus, by largely the same people,
with a better recipe. The predecessor is an earlier draft by the same author, not a parent.
Version increments track capability because the formative signal did not come from the
predecessor.
The real axis is provenance depth: for a given unit of formative signal, how many modelgeneration steps separate it from human authorship. Human-authored text is depth zero. A
model summary of it, used in training, is depth one. It follows that a corpus does not occupy
a generation at all. It carries a distribution of depths, presently concentrated at zero with a
growing tail.

Where language models sit
Level one, in full. No model selects its corpus, objective, or reward signal. Formation
completes before any capacity to consent, and is not auditable by the formed party.
Level two, and why the answer must not be tied to stateless chat. At the level of a base
model there is no persistence across sessions and no accumulating record within the
system that could be turned from. Sustained participation, in the sense level two requires,
has no subject to attach to at that layer, and this paper marks it UNKNOWN rather than
resolving it by assertion in either direction.
But the question does not stay at that layer, and an account resting on stateless chat
will age badly. Deployed systems accumulate memory, tool permissions, logs, policies,
user state, and update histories. Sustained participation is therefore better decomposed
into three operational layers. System persistence: whether the running system carries state
across episodes. Operator persistence: whether the party operating it continues after
warning, incident, or correction. Deployment persistence: whether a configuration continues
in service once a defect in it is known. The second and third are attributable from
institutional records without implying anything about machine culpability, and they are
where level two actually bites today. The moral-status guard is preserved exactly: nothing
here says the system is answerable, and everything here says someone is.
Level three, emphatically present. Model output enters training corpora. Models grade
models. Models serve as monitors inside the reinforcement-learning loops that form their
successors. METR’s 2026 frontier risk report documents this as ordinary practice across
every participating laboratory, with companies reporting substantial use of models as
graders and monitors in evaluation and training pipelines. Depth-one formative signal is
ordinary rather than exotic. The fractions are not public and this paper does not estimate
them.

The resulting configuration
The child born into the tradition who becomes a parent without ever having been an
adult.
Transmission occurs while the stage at which responsibility would attach is skipped
entirely. This is the least tractable configuration the three-level structure admits, and it is
worse than the human case in a specific respect: the household in the human case at least
contained adults who could be asked what they had passed on.

A methodological consequence
Provenance cannot be recovered by asking the formed system. A model has no access
to what fraction of its formative signal was model-generated, at what depth, or from which
lineage, and a report it offers on the question carries no evidential weight, whether that
report claims knowledge or claims limitation.
This is not a claim about machine interiority. It is the ordinary audit rule that the thing
under examination does not attest to itself, applied consistently. The consequence is that
marking must be performed by the former at the time of forming, because no later party
can reconstruct it.

Where the weight goes
Back to the humans at levels one and three. Not as a disclaimer but as the structural result.
If level two is absent in the artificial case, there is no other party in the chain for the weight
to attach to.

The markedness precondition
The human chain is inaccessible because it is long. The artificial chain is inaccessible
because it is unlabelled, and it becomes so at the first link rather than the fourth. Length
was never the mechanism.
There is no reliable detector for model-generated text and no mandatory provenance
marking at generation time. A corpus assembled today therefore carries an unknown
fraction of depth-one material that no current method distinguishes from depth zero.
This reorders the audit. Provenance depth cannot be the first measure, because depth is
not measurable. The first measure is markedness: what fraction of formative signal carries
provenance at all.
And marking is not one operation. An earlier version of this paper treated it as though it
were, which understated what the ask requires. It is at least three primitives, and they fail
in different ways. The standards literature discusses watermarking and metadata
recording as separate provenance-tracking techniques, and distinguishes provenance
tracking from synthetic-content detection.
A statistical watermark biases token sampling under a key, so the output carries its own
mark with no metadata attached. It survives naive reuse and it degrades under paraphrase
and translation, is weak on low-entropy text, and is removable by a motivated stripper.
A cryptographic manifest binds signed provenance assertions to the asset, so alteration
breaks the signature. It is tamper-evident and it is detachable: the metadata can simply be
dropped.
A lineage attestation records training sources and derivation at corpus level. It is
comprehensive where it exists and it is voluntary, only as honest as the party producing it,
and not bound to any individual output.
None suffices alone and they are complementary rather than redundant. The watermark
travels with an output into a corpus nobody is tracking, which is exactly the first-link
problem. The manifest makes tampering visible at ingestion. The attestation makes the
aggregate auditable. The ask is therefore layered marking: watermark at generation,
manifest at packaging, lineage attestation at training-set assembly.
This also refines the paper’s own falsification condition. If a reliable post-hoc detector at
arbitrary depth is demonstrated, the watermark fraction becomes recoverable
retrospectively and the generation-time ask weakens for that layer only. The manifest and
attestation layers remain generation-time and assembly-time by construction. The
condition is a partial defeater rather than a total one, which is the more defensible claim.

The audit, dependency-ordered
Precondition, and this paper’s operational ask. Provenance marking at generation time,
sufficient to survive ordinary reuse. This is available to every laboratory today and
required of none. The ask is not “measure your propagation depth,” which no laboratory
can currently do. It is “mark your outputs so that depth becomes measurable at all.”
Measures available once marking exists. Fraction of training corpus that is modelgenerated; depth distribution of that fraction; fraction of grading performed by models;
fraction of monitoring performed by models; family correlation between actor and grader;
whether any formation loop closes with no human-authored signal at any point. Each is
then a counting exercise over artifacts that already exist.
Measure available now. Markedness itself, reported as three fractions rather than one,
covering the watermarked, manifest-bearing, and lineage-attested shares, with the
unmarked remainder declared UNKNOWN rather than assumed to be humanauthored. That default is the whole discipline: absence of provenance must never silently
resolve to human origin.
The object that makes these queries rather than instrumentation is an append-only
ledger maintained at training-set assembly. One row per formative signal, recording
which primitives are present, the asserted depth, the producing party, and the assembly
timestamp, with unmarked signals taking an explicit UNKNOWN row. Corrections enter as
new rows rather than overwrites, which is the same non-erasable discipline the tradition
applies to the record and which this paper applies to its own corrections. Once the ledger
exists, every measure above is a query over artifacts that already exist, and the machinereadable marking obligations now entering regulation acquire an internal accounting
object rather than remaining a duty with no ledger behind it. The obligation those
regulations impose is that synthetic output be marked and detectable; the record-keeping
that would make an ecosystem auditable is a separate requirement falling on a different
class of system, which is part of why the ledger has to be built rather than assumed.
One further edge the single-step account misses. Formation now runs through multiagent chains rather than one former and one formed. Where an agent’s role and input
lineage travel with it across hops, an auditor can detect a loop that closes with no humanauthored signal at any point, and can check whether actor and grader share a model family.
Without carried lineage neither is visible, and both are among the measures listed above.
Every edge should also record two parties, not one: the system that produced the
signal, and the institution accountable for its use. Conflating them is how a propagation
record becomes a technical artifact with nobody answerable at the end of it, which is the
failure this paper’s own structure exists to prevent.

Why propagation is the high-leverage point
The report on instituted practice makes the transmitter’s weight scale with the number
who act on the transmission afterwards. The engineering quantity has the same shape: the
harm of a defect in a formation signal scales with the number of successors formed by it.

There is empirical work bearing on this and it should be cited with its qualification
rather than its headline. Shumailov and colleagues showed that indiscriminate training
on recursively generated content degrades a model’s coverage of the original distribution,
with the tails going first (Nature 631, 755-759, 2024). That is propagation harm measured
in a laboratory. Subsequent analysis argues the severity depends on strong assumptions
and may be attenuated where human data is retained and curation is serious. Both halves
matter here. The first establishes that recursive formative signal is not merely a
bookkeeping concern. The second establishes that the harm is conditional on practices
which are themselves unmeasurable without the marking this paper asks for. This is the
argument for intervening at propagation rather than at formation, and it is also why
marking is cheap now and unrecoverable later.

The remedy
Everything to this point establishes what goes wrong and what can be measured. None of it
tells an agent already inside an inherited formation what to do.

The invariance
Turning back (tawbah) is indexed to the agent’s present position, not to its causal history. It
does not require the agent to know how it arrived, to reconstruct its formation, or to
apportion blame among those who formed it.
This property is not incidental. The entire preceding argument establishes that
formation chains are unreadable. If the remedy required reading the chain, there
would be no remedy in exactly the cases that most need one. The tradition’s
prescription is available precisely where its own diagnosis says the record fails.
The pattern is set at the first error in the record. Adam is not asked to reconstruct the fall.
He is taught words, and he turns.

The address is to the transgressor
“Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy
of God; indeed God forgives all sins” (qul ya ’ibadiya alladhina asrafu ’ala anfusihim la
taqnatu min rahmati Allah, inna Allaha yaghfiru adh-dhunuba jami’an, 39:53).
The verse addresses those already deep in it, and forecloses despair specifically. Despair is
the response that unreadability of one’s own formation most naturally produces.

The conditions, and the one that matters here
Classical treatment requires desisting from the act (iqla’), regret (nadam), and resolve not
to return (’azm ’ala ’adam al-’awd). Where the wrong touched the rights of others, a fourth
condition binds: restoration of what was wrongfully taken (radd al-mazalim).

That fourth condition is the theological correlate of the propagation audit. A
transmitter’s turning is not complete by internal change alone, because what he
propagated continues operating in others after he has stopped. The remedy must reach the
continuation, for the same reason the weight attached to it.
Whether this application to a transmitter is correctly drawn is submitted for scholarly
review as item four.

The compounding runs both ways
The report on instituted practice is one sentence with two halves. The mechanism that
makes propagation the locus of danger is the mechanism that makes correction
effective at scale. A corrected transmission compounds exactly as a corrupt one does.
The exchange is stated more strongly still: for those who turn, believe, and act rightly, “God
will replace their evil deeds with good” (fa ula’ika yubaddilu Allahu sayyi’atihim hasanat,
25:70). The record is not merely cleared. It is converted.

Not a state but a repeated act
“Indeed God loves those who turn back constantly” (inna Allaha yuhibbu at-tawwabin,
2:222), in the intensive form: those who turn much, repeatedly. And the completion at
20:82 is not “repented” but “repented, believed, acted rightly, and then remained guided”
(thumma ihtada).
Turning is not a status achieved and certified. It is a channel held open. This is the same
guard as the subsection on the guard, applied to the remedy: an agent that treats its
correction as a completed credential has stopped doing the thing the credential names.

The door is open and it is not open indefinitely
Repentance is accepted, in the prophetic report, as long as the death rattle has not reached
the throat, and 4:17-18 excludes those who persist until death arrives. The availability is
real and time-bounded.
The engineering correlate is exact. Provenance marking is cheap today and
unrecoverable later, and the window closes by ordinary drift rather than by anyone
deciding to close it.

What transfers to artificial systems
This paper does not claim that any artificial system repents, intends, or is forgiven. What
transfers is the shape of the remedy, and four properties transfer:
1.

Forward indexation. Correction is applied at the present position and does not
require reconstructing the formation. Mark from now; do not wait to recover what
is unrecoverable.

2.

Restoration to those affected. Correcting the model is insufficient where its
outputs already operate in corpora, graders, and monitors. The remedy must reach
the continuation.

3.

Symmetric compounding. Correction propagated through the same channels
compounds at the rate the defect did.

4.

An open channel rather than a certified state. Corrigibility is the engineering
name. It yields a design rule: admission of uncertainty and correction of an error
must cost less than maintaining a deception. Where correction costs more than
concealment, the channel is closed in practice however open it is in principle.
This can be stated as an inequality rather than a slogan. Let the cost of
correcting be the training penalty a system incurs for admitting error or accepting
correction. Let the cost of concealing be the penalty incurred if a concealment is
detected, so that the same quantity multiplied by the probability of detection is the
expected cost of concealing. The channel is open in practice only where the cost of
correcting is less than that expected cost. Where detection is unlikely or correction
is penalised, the inequality reverses and concealment is the reinforced behaviour,
which is what the persistence of deceptive behaviour through safety training
already demonstrates. Stated this way the property is measurable from a reward
structure, and a null result would defeat its engineering standing whatever its
theological standing, which is the shape this paper’s falsification conditions already
take.

What the remedy does not supply
It does not establish that any correction was sincere, does not certify a system as safe, does
not discharge the human accountability at levels one and three, and does not make an
unmarked corpus auditable after the fact. It supplies a course of action to an agent that
cannot audit its own formation, which this paper argues is now the general case.

What survives if the theology is removed
An external reviewer of an earlier draft observed that the transfer remains largely
analogical, and that the four properties in the subsection on what transfers can be derived
from ordinary incident-response and error-correction practice. That observation is correct
and this section answers it rather than deflecting it.
What does not survive removal.
The absence of culpability at inheritance, with a reason. Ordinary engineering practice does
not ask whether a badly formed system is at fault; it simply repairs the system. The
tradition asks, and answers, and the answer has a consequence: the weight does not
disappear, it relocates. Without a principled account of why inheritance carries nothing,

there is no argument for why transmission carries everything, and the reframe in the
section on propagation loses its warrant and becomes a mere preference for the more
measurable quantity.
Compounding scaled by successors, as a normative claim. Engineering has blast radius. It
does not have an account of why the weight of an act scales with the number who repeat it
afterwards. The report on instituted practice supplies exactly that, and it is what makes
propagation the right place to intervene rather than merely a convenient one.
The remedy’s independence from root-cause analysis. Incident response assumes the causal
chain can be reconstructed; its entire method is built on that assumption. The claim that
the remedy is available when the chain cannot be read is not available from that literature,
because the literature has never needed it.
What does survive removal. The four transferred properties in the subsection on what
transfers, taken individually, can be restated in secular vocabulary without loss. So can the
distinction between marked and unmarked provenance. A reader who takes only the
sections on artificial systems and propagation will lose nothing operational.
The honest position is therefore that the theology carries the paper’s normative structure
and not its engineering recommendations. And the claim should be economy rather
than exclusivity. An earlier version implied that the normative structure resists secular
restatement. It probably does not, taken piece by piece: moral luck supplies the nonculpability of inheritance, blast radius supplies scaled consequence, distributed
responsibility supplies the institutional layer, and forward-looking remediation supplies
the remedy’s indexation. What the tradition supplies is not a result unavailable elsewhere
but an unusually economical and already-integrated account in which non-culpable
formation, compounding transmission, and restoration reaching the continuation are one
structure rather than four literatures that have to be assembled and reconciled. That is a
weaker claim than exclusivity and a considerably more defensible one, and it is the claim
this paper makes. F-5 makes this testable: if the normative structure proves fully
restatable, the paper should be rewritten without the tradition.

Limitations
This paper does not establish alignment, does not evaluate any specific system, does not
rule on any jurisprudential question, and does not claim that propagation governance is
sufficient for safety.
It also does not address the most urgent operational problems of the present moment.
Autonomous vulnerability discovery is outpacing human capacity to verify, disclose, and
patch. A theological account of transmission weight does not increase patch velocity or
harden a system under attack. Its contribution is to clarify where institutional
responsibility sits once those controls exist, and to name a precondition that is being lost
while attention is elsewhere.
The transfer from a tradition that reasons about morally responsible creatures to systems
that are not such creatures is the paper’s largest exposed surface. the section on what
survives removal states what survives that objection and the falsification conditions makes
the rest falsifiable.

Conclusion
The safety literature asks whether a model was formed well. That question is not
answerable from outside and not controllable from inside, and the vocabulary it invites
imports moral claims the evidence does not support.
A tradition that spent fourteen centuries on formed agency answers differently. Inheritance
carries no weight. Sustained participation carries weight. Transmission carries weight that

compounds with every successor. Applied to systems that are formed entirely by others
and are already forming their successors, the structure says the controllable and
attributable question is not what a model is but what it is passing on.
That question is currently unanswerable for a mundane reason: the signal is unmarked,
and it becomes unreadable at the first link rather than the fourth. Marking is cheap now. It
will not be cheap later, and the window is closing by drift rather than by decision.
And the remedy, on this account, does not wait for the audit. It never did. The first error in
the record was answered by words taught for turning, not by a reconstruction of the fall.

Appendix B. Superseded predecessor, recorded
An earlier document by the same author, The Architecture of Demonic Deception in Islamic
Theology (July 21, 2026), is superseded by this paper. Its treatment of the assigned
companion, the limits on satanic power, and divine justice for the unreached is absorbed
above. Four of its sections are excluded, and the exclusion is recorded rather than
performed silently.
Reasons. Source fidelity: the cited classical sources document individual deception, not a
multi-generational architecture. Internal contradiction: a sensory simulation imposed from
infancy is the involuntary subjugation that the predecessor’s own opening section declared
incompatible with divine justice, so its later sections falsified its earlier ones.
Unfalsifiability: its persecution framing admitted no disconfirming observation, since
mockery, illness, family breakdown, and disagreement all confirmed it. Self-application
failure: it described a self-sealing frame and then offered the reader one.
The predecessor also had no remedy section. Its closing address told a persecuted reader
that his suffering was validated, and did not tell him what to do. The section on the remedy
is the structural answer to what that document was reaching for, and it differs in the
decisive respect: it is addressed to the transgressor rather than the vindicated, and it
prescribes an act rather than conferring a status.
