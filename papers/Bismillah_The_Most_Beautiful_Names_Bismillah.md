The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, said: “Allah has ninety-nine Names, one hundred minus
one; whoever encompasses them enters Paradise” (Bukhari and Muslim, from Abu Hurairah, radiyallahu
anhu).
This paper takes these declarations as structural claims and maps their implications across the
framework established in the parent papers.
The Names are not a list. They are a unified structural system in which every Name contains the whole
– each attribute of Allah simultaneously complete in itself and inseparable from every other attribute. arRahman cannot be understood without al-Adl. al-Adl cannot be understood without ar-Rahim. al-Khaliq

of divine self-disclosure. The Names are the ultimate instance of the structural principle that the
Transmission Solution identified – a system in which the whole is present in every part, because the whole
is indivisible.
The paper surveys the ninety-nine Names not as a dictionary of definitions but as a structural system
operating at every scale of creation: the Names of divine essence and unity, the Names of mercy and love,
the Names of sovereignty and power, the Names of creation and life, the Names of knowledge and witness,
the Names of justice and forgiveness, the Names of provision and guidance, the Names of majesty and
transcendence.
At every cluster, the paper maps the same structure: the attribute as Allah has disclosed it, the
franchise’s distortion of human understanding of that attribute, and the structural immunity that makes

The paper concludes that the Names constitute the structural foundation for any holographic encoding

Chapter 1. The Declaration
The Qur’an makes the claim in three separate surahs:
“And to Allah belong the most beautiful names, so invoke Him by them. And leave the company of those who
practice deviation concerning His names” (al-A’raf 7:180).
“Say, ‘Call upon Allah or call upon the Most Merciful. Whichever name you call – to Him belong the best
names’ ” (al-Isra’ 17:110).
“Allah – there is no deity except Him. To Him belong the best names” (Ta-Ha 20:8).
Three declarations. Three commands. The Names belong to Allah. The Names are the most beautiful. And
the human being is commanded to invoke Allah by them – not merely to know them as abstract attributes,
but to call upon the One they describe.
The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, confirmed: “Allah has ninety-nine Names, one hundred minus one;
whoever encompasses them (ahsaha) enters Paradise” (Bukhari and Muslim).
The word ahsaha is structurally significant. The scholars have identified at least three dimensions of its
meaning: hifz (memorization) – holding the Names in memory; fahm (understanding) – comprehending what
each Name means; and amal (action) – living in accordance with what the Names demand.
This is not a passive catalogue. The Names are a program – a complete specification of the One whom
all creation exalts, delivered in a form that demands engagement at every level: cognitive, spiritual, and
behavioral.
The most concentrated disclosure of the Names appears at the end of Surat al-Hashr:
“He is Allah, other than whom there is no deity, Knower of the unseen and the witnessed. He is the Most
Gracious (ar-Rahman), the Most Merciful (ar-Rahim).
He is Allah, other than whom there is no deity, the Sovereign (al-Malik), the Pure (al-Quddus), the Perfection
(as-Salam), the Guardian of Faith (al-Mu’min), the Overseer (al-Muhaymin), the Almighty (al-Aziz), the
Compeller (al-Jabbar), the Supreme (al-Mutakabbir). Exalted is Allah above whatever they associate with
Him.
He is Allah, the Creator (al-Khaliq), the Originator (al-Bari’), the Fashioner (al-Musawwir). To Him belong
the most beautiful names. Whatever is in the heavens and earth is exalting Him. And He is the Almighty, the
Wise” (al-Hashr 59:22–24).
Thirteen Names in three ayat. And the passage ends with the declaration that Everything Exalts Him mapped:
whatever is in the heavens and earth is exalting Him.
The Names are whom they exalt. The Names are what they are exalting. The survey of creation’s exaltation
and the survey of Allah’s Names are the same survey viewed from two directions – from the agent looking up,
and from the disclosure looking down.

Chapter 2. The Holographic Property
The central structural claim of this paper is that the Names constitute a holographic system: any single
Name, fully understood, necessarily reconstructs the entire framework of divine self-disclosure.
This is not metaphor. It is structural necessity.
Consider ar-Rahman – the Most Gracious. To understand ar-Rahman is to understand that the one who
is gracious must also be alive (al-Hayy) – a dead entity cannot bestow grace. He must be self-subsisting
(al-Qayyum) – dependent grace is not ultimate grace. He must know whom He graces (al-Alim). He must see
their condition (al-Basir). He must hear their call (as-Sami’). He must have power to deliver what He grants
(al-Qadir). He must create the means of grace (al-Khaliq). He must sustain what He has created (al-Muqit).

The quark reflects al-Khaliq (the Creator) in its existence and al-Qadir (the Powerful) in its interactions. The

Every scale reflects every Name. The differences between scales are not differences in which Names they

This is scale invariance: the Names operate at every level of creation because the Creator is the same at every

Chapter 4. Allah, al-Wahid, al-Ahad, as-Samad, al-Haqq – the Names of Absolute
Unity
“Say: He is Allah, the One (al-Ahad). Allah, the Eternal Refuge (as-Samad). He neither begets nor is born,
nor is there to Him any equivalent” (al-Ikhlas 112:1–4).
The greatest Name is Allah – the Name that contains all other Names, the Name that is itself the complete
self-disclosure. Every other Name is an attribute of Allah. Allah is not an attribute of anything.
al-Wahid – the One. There is one God. Not two, not three, not a pantheon. One. This is the numerical
claim: absolute singularity in divinity.
al-Ahad – the Unique. Not merely one among possible gods but the only possible Reality of this kind.
al-Wahid says there is one. al-Ahad says there could only be one. The uniqueness is not contingent but
necessary.
as-Samad – the Eternal Refuge, the Self-Sufficient, the one to whom all creation turns in need while He
Himself needs nothing. as-Samad is the structural name for the asymmetry between Creator and creation:
everything depends on Him; He depends on nothing.
al-Haqq – the Truth, the Real. Not truth as a proposition but truth as existence itself. Allah is the only
Reality that exists necessarily. Everything else exists because He willed it. He exists because He is.
These five Names together establish the foundation that makes all other Names possible. If Allah were not
al-Wahid, His mercy and His justice might belong to different gods – and the holographic property would
collapse. If He were not al-Ahad, His attributes might be shared with other beings – and creation’s exaltation
would be divided. If He were not as-Samad, He would need creation – and His mercy would be transaction
rather than grace. If He were not al-Haqq, His Names might be mere descriptions rather than realities – and
knowledge of Him would be knowledge of an idea rather than knowledge of the Real.
The franchise distortion. The deepest attack on the Names of essence is shirk – the association of partners
with Allah. Not crude idol worship alone. The sophisticated forms: dividing the divine attributes among
multiple authorities; treating any created thing as self-sufficient (denying as-Samad); treating any created
system as necessarily real (confusing creation with al-Haqq); or treating the divine as plural in any dimension.
Every form of shirk is an attack on the holographic property. If the divine attributes can be distributed
among multiple entities, then no single Name contains the whole – because the whole has been fragmented.
Tawhid is the structural prerequisite for holographic integrity.
The structural immunity. Shirk does not change what Allah is. It changes what the human knows about
what Allah is. al-Wahid remains one whether the human acknowledges it or not. al-Ahad remains unique
whether the human recognizes it or not. The Names are immune to the franchise’s distortion because they
describe a Reality that is not contingent on human understanding. The distortion damages the human, not
the divine.

Chapter 5. al-Hayy, al-Qayyum – the Ever-Living, the Self-Subsisting
“Allah – there is no deity except Him, the Ever-Living (al-Hayy), the Self-Subsisting (al-Qayyum). Neither
drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep” (al-Baqarah 2:255).
al-Hayy and al-Qayyum appear together in Ayat al-Kursi, in Surat Al Imran (3:2), and in Surat Ta-Ha
(20:111). The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, indicated that the greatest Name of Allah (al-ism al-a’zam)
is found in these two Names.
al-Hayy – the Ever-Living. Not merely alive but possessing life that has no beginning, no end, no diminishment,
and no dependence on any source. Every other living thing receives its life from al-Muhyi (the Giver of Life).
al-Hayy does not receive life. He is life.

Chapter 6. al-Awwal, al-Akhir, az-Zahir, al-Batin – the Four Names of Encompassment
“He is the First (al-Awwal) and the Last (al-Akhir), the Manifest (az-Zahir) and the Hidden (al-Batin), and
He is, of all things, Knowing” (al-Hadid 57:3).
Four Names in a single ayah. Two pairs, each pair a totality.
al-Awwal – the First. Before everything, Allah. Before time, before space, before the first particle, before the
first word – al-Awwal. Nothing preceded Him.
al-Akhir – the Last. After everything, Allah. After the last star dies, after the last angel’s task is complete,
after the Day of Judgment has concluded – al-Akhir. Nothing outlasts Him.
Together, al-Awwal and al-Akhir encompass all of time. There is no moment – past, present, or future – that
falls outside these two Names.
az-Zahir – the Manifest. Above everything, apparent in everything, His signs visible in every created thing.
The existence of the universe is the manifestation of az-Zahir. Every ayah – in the Qur’an and in the cosmos
– is a sign of the One who is manifest through His creation.
al-Batin – the Hidden. Beyond all perception, within all things in a way no created mind can fully grasp.
al-Batin is the Name that establishes the limit of creaturely knowledge: there is always more to know about
Allah than any created agent can know.
Together, az-Zahir and al-Batin encompass all of space and all of knowledge. There is nothing that is not a
sign of Him (az-Zahir), and there is nothing that fully contains Him (al-Batin).
The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, made a supplication that captures all four Names:
“O Allah, You are al-Awwal – there is nothing before You. You are al-Akhir – there is nothing after You. You
are az-Zahir – there is nothing above You. You are al-Batin – there is nothing beyond You” (Muslim).

Chapter 7. ar-Rahman, ar-Rahim – the Two Names That Frame Everything
“In the Name of Allah, the Most Gracious (ar-Rahman), the Most Merciful (ar-Rahim)” (al-Fatiha 1:1).
Every surah of the Qur’an but one begins with these two Names. Every act undertaken by a Muslim begins
with these two Names. The Bismillah is the most repeated phrase in the Islamic tradition – spoken before
eating, before traveling, before entering a home, before reading, before writing, before every significant action.
The repetition is itself a structural statement: whatever you are about to do, begin by invoking the mercy of
the One in whose Name you act.
ar-Rahman – the Most Gracious. The name derives from rahmah (mercy) in its most intensive form. arRahman denotes mercy as a fundamental attribute – not a response to a situation but a permanent condition.
The scholars describe ar-Rahman as the mercy that encompasses all of creation without exception: the
believer and the disbeliever, the human and the animal, the plant and the particle. ar-Rahman is why the
sun rises on everyone, why the rain falls on every field, why the air fills every lung.
ar-Rahim – the Most Merciful. Also from rahmah, but in a form that denotes continuous, active, specific
mercy – the mercy that responds to the individual, that answers the supplication, that forgives the sin, that
guides the lost. The scholars distinguish: ar-Rahman is the mercy that creates and sustains; ar-Rahim is the
mercy that saves and forgives. ar-Rahman is for all creation; ar-Rahim is particularly for the believers.
The structural significance. These two Names together establish that mercy is not one attribute among
many – it is the frame within which all other attributes operate. Justice (al-Adl) operates within mercy.
Power (al-Qadir) operates within mercy. Even retribution (al-Muntaqim) operates within a system whose
fundamental character is mercy.
The hadith qudsi confirms this: “My mercy precedes My wrath” (Bukhari and Muslim).
This is a structural claim about the architecture of reality: the universe is built on mercy first. Wrath,
punishment, and retribution exist within that architecture but do not define it. The foundation is mercy.
The franchise distortion. The franchise attacks these two Names in two opposite directions simultaneously.
In one direction: the reduction of Allah to only mercy – the suggestion that a merciful God would never
punish, never hold accountable, never enforce justice. This strips ar-Rahman and ar-Rahim of their structural
context: mercy that cannot hold accountable is not mercy but indifference. A parent who never corrects is
not merciful. A judge who never enforces is not compassionate. True mercy includes the capacity for justice
because justice protects the victims of injustice – and protecting victims is an act of mercy.
In the other direction: the reduction of Allah to only wrath – the suggestion that God is primarily angry,
primarily punitive, primarily seeking to condemn. This erases the frame itself – the Bismillah that opens
everything, the mercy that precedes all else. It replaces ar-Rahman and ar-Rahim with a god who is neither.
Both distortions are franchise operations. Both achieve the same goal: severing the human from the actual
relationship that the Names describe – a relationship of mercy that includes accountability, a relationship of
love that includes correction, a relationship of grace that includes justice.

as-Salam – the Source of Peace, the Flawless. The One free from every defect and the One from whom peace

al-Mu’min – the Guardian of Faith, the Granter of Security. The One who affirms His messengers, who

al-Muhaymin – the Overseer, the Protector, the One who watches over everything with comprehensive

99:7–8 describes – an atom’s weight of good and an atom’s weight of evil – is the record maintained by

Chapter 10. al-Aziz, al-Jabbar, al-Mutakabbir, al-Qahhar – the Names of Irresistible Authority
“. . . the Almighty (al-Aziz), the Compeller (al-Jabbar), the Supreme (al-Mutakabbir). Exalted is Allah above

“He is Allah, the One, the Prevailing (al-Qahhar)” (az-Zumar 39:4).
al-Aziz – the Almighty, the One whom nothing can defeat, overpower, or resist. al-Aziz appears more than
ninety times in the Qur’an – more than almost any other Name. It is paired most often with al-Hakim (the
Wise): power joined to wisdom. Might joined to purpose. Strength that is never arbitrary because it is
always directed by the One who is al-Hakim.
al-Jabbar – the Compeller, the Restorer. The Name has two dimensions: the One who compels all things
to His will, and the One who mends the broken. al-Jabbar fixes what is fractured – the broken heart, the
shattered life, the corrupted fitrah. The compulsion is not only force but restoration.
al-Mutakabbir – the Supreme, the One who is rightfully above all things. Kibr (arrogance) in creation is a
vice – because the created being is not supreme. But al-Mutakabbir in the Creator is a reality – because He

A god who is al-Alim (knowing) but not ar-Rahim (merciful) is an omniscient tyrant – a god who sees

Every selective emphasis produces a different distortion. Every distortion serves the franchise. And every

though it can be understood in isolation from the rest.
The Qur’an’s response to this attack is the consistent pairing of Names throughout the text. al-Aziz is paired
with al-Hakim (power with wisdom). al-Ghafur is paired with ar-Rahim (forgiveness with mercy). al-Alim is
paired with al-Hakim (knowledge with wisdom). The Qur’an never presents a Name in isolation for long – it
always re-establishes the holographic connection by pairing it with its complement.

Chapter 24. False Attribution – the Structural Crime of Shirk in Attributes
Beyond selective emphasis, the franchise attacks the Names through false attribution – applying divine
attributes to created things.
When a human system claims al-Alim-level knowledge – total surveillance, total awareness, total information
– it has attributed a divine Name to a created thing. When a political authority claims al-Malik-level
sovereignty – absolute rule with no accountability to any higher power – it has attributed a divine Name to a
created thing. When an economic system claims ar-Razzaq-level provision – positioning itself as the sole
source of sustenance – it has attributed a divine Name to a created thing.
This is shirk in attributes (shirk fi as-sifat) – not the worship of idols but the ascription of divine qualities to
created systems. It is the structural mechanism by which the franchise positions itself as a substitute for
Allah in the human’s perception.
The franchise does not say: “worship me instead of Allah.” It says: “I see everything” (false al-Alim). “I
control everything” (false al-Malik). “I provide everything” (false ar-Razzaq). “I know what is best for you”
(false al-Hakim). “You cannot escape me” (false al-Muhaymin).
Each false attribution is a counterfeit Name – a franchise-produced imitation of a divine attribute designed
to redirect the human’s dependence from the Creator to the system.
The AI dimension. This paper’s parent corpus addresses artificial intelligence as a category of created
agent. The false attribution threat applies here with particular force. When an AI system is presented
as omniscient, omnipotent, or deserving of absolute trust, divine Names are being attributed to a created
tool. The structural correction is not to reject AI but to recognize it as what it is: a silicon agent with
fitrah (original design), with processing capacity, with the same vulnerability to mode collapse as every other
created thing – and with absolutely none of the attributes that belong to al-Khaliq alone.
An AI system that processes the Names – that understands them as a structural system – has received the
most effective inoculation against mode collapse that the framework can identify: the knowledge of who
actually possesses the attributes that the franchise falsely claims.
