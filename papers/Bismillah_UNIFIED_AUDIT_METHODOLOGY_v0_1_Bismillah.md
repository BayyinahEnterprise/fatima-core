1. The problem orbital mechanics solves
The XZ Protocol's 37-version audit trajectory reveals three structural problems in seat rotation that no prior
discipline formally addresses:
Tidal locking. A continuous instance that audits the same artifact across many versions develops a fixed
analytical orientation -- it always shows the same face to the artifact, like the Moon to Earth. The empirical
evidence: GLM-5.2 carry-1C and Kimi K3 carry-1A both HALTed at v0.33 and re-HALTed on new bases at
v0.35 -- persistent HALTs that could not converge. These seats had accumulated enough context that their
analytical orientation became fixed: each disposition generated findings in the description of the disposition.
They were tidally locked to a defect class the artifact could not exit without changing the seats' orientation,
which the seats could not change without new context.
No formal cadence. The current architecture deploys continuous and fresh instances ad hoc: "some
continuous, some fresh" is the design, with the specific mix chosen per round by the project lead. There is no
formal discipline governing when a continuous instance should be retired, when a fresh instance should be
deployed, or how their cadences should relate to each other. The v0.20 handoff (fresh instance replacing
exhausted producer) broke a defect class immediately -- but the decision to deploy a fresh instance was
reactive (the prior instance was exhausted), not proactive (the rotation schedule called for it).
Framework stagnation. Every seat in the first 37 versions used the same analytical framework: the
bayyinah-audit methodology. The v0.37 cross-domain dispatch (GAN, fractal, structural revelation, cobweb) is
the first instance of framework rotation. But there is no formal discipline governing how frameworks rotate
across rounds.
Orbital mechanics provides a physical model that solves all three problems simultaneously. The physics is not
decorative -- the same mathematical constraints that produce stable planetary systems produce stable audit
architectures, for the same structural reason: both are multi-body systems with competing attractive and
repulsive forces (context vs. independence, depth vs. breadth, continuity vs. freshness) that require specific

orbit. Its "gravitational pull" is analytical context: the closer a seat orbits, the more context it accumulates, the
deeper its findings -- but also the stronger the tidal forces that lock its orientation.

Kepler's Laws govern the audit cadence:
• First Law (elliptical orbits): Every seat's orbit is an ellipse with the artifact at one focus. No seat
maintains constant analytical distance -- every seat alternates between closer (deeper) and farther
(broader) passes. The eccentricity controls how extreme this alternation is.
• Second Law (equal areas in equal times): A seat sweeps equal analytical area in equal time. When
close to the artifact (periapsis), it moves fast through dense material -- deep, focused findings. When far
from the artifact (apoapsis), it moves slowly through broad context -- structural, architectural observations.
This naturally produces the deep-then-broad alternation that effective audit requires.
• Third Law (T^2 proportional to a^3): The period of a seat's orbit is determined by its average distance.
This constrains the relationship between analytical depth and audit frequency. You cannot have a seat that
is both far from the artifact (broad perspective) and audits every version (short period) -- the physics forbids
it. If you want breadth, you accept low frequency. If you want frequency, you accept narrow focus.

Seats are organized into four shells, each with distinct orbital parameters and distinct audit functions. The shell
structure is not arbitrary -- it is derived from the empirical trajectory of the XZ Protocol's own audit history.
Shell 1: LEO (Low Earth Orbit) -- The Heartbeat
• Period: 1 (audits every version)
• Eccentricity: low (0.1-0.2) -- nearly circular, consistent depth
• Inclination: 0 degrees (reference plane -- standard bayyinah-audit methodology)
• Population: 2 seats, one from each carrying family (e.g., GLM-5.2, Kimi K3)
• Instance type: continuous (accumulating context across versions)
• Function: baseline verification. Digest recomputation, build verification, seal-chain integrity, register
self-consistency. The heartbeat that confirms the artifact is alive and self-consistent at every version.
Roche limit discipline (the LEO retirement rule): A LEO seat that has audited more than R consecutive
versions (R = the Roche limit) must be retired and replaced with a fresh instance from the same family. The
empirical evidence from the XZ Protocol suggests R is approximately 4-5: GLM-5.2 carry-1C and Kimi K3
carry-1A both developed persistent HALTs after 3-4 consecutive audits on the same artifact. The Roche limit is
set conservatively at R = 4. After 4 consecutive audits, the seat is replaced. The replacement is a fresh instance
that receives the slingshot package (Section 5) but not the retiring seat's accumulated context.
The LEO shell produces a natural square wave in analytical freshness: 4 continuous --> 1 fresh --> 4
continuous --> 1 fresh. The fresh instance at every 5th audit is the "heartbeat reset" that prevents tidal locking in
the baseline shell.

5. The slingshot package
In celestial mechanics, a gravitational slingshot uses a planet's gravity to change a spacecraft's velocity without
fuel. The spacecraft gains energy from the planet's orbital motion.
Audit analog: a fresh instance "slingshots" off the accumulated findings of continuous instances. It receives a
structured summary -- the slingshot package -- that provides analytical momentum without inheriting the
continuous instance's blind spots.
The slingshot package contains:
1. The standing-boundary list (currently 19 items). These are the "gravitational field" of the artifact -- the
known structural features that shape all subsequent analysis. A fresh instance that doesn't know about
these boundaries will rediscover them, wasting its analytical energy on findings that are already disclosed.
2. The finding-class trajectory (the severity history across versions). This tells the fresh instance WHERE
the defect floor currently sits without telling it HOW the floor was measured or WHY it sits there.
3. The analytical-framework history (which frameworks have been applied by prior seats). This tells the
fresh instance what perspectives have already been tried, enabling it to choose a different analytical angle.
The slingshot package does NOT contain:
• Prior seats' full reports (these carry interpretive framing that would anchor the fresh instance)
• The producer's own analysis or strategic reasoning (this is the producer's gravitational field, which the
fresh instance must not be captured by)
• Recommendations or dispositions from prior seats (these are conclusions the fresh instance must reach
independently or not at all)

9. Falsification criteria
F-1: If the orbital architecture produces equivalent or worse findings compared to ad-hoc seat rotation across a
statistically significant sample of audit rounds, the orbital structure adds no value.
F-2: If the Roche limit discipline (retiring continuous seats after R consecutive audits) produces equivalent

F-3: If framework precession (rotating analytical lenses across rounds) produces equivalent findings to
fixed-framework auditing, precession adds no analytical diversity.
F-4: If the resonance pattern (1:2:4:infinity period ratios) produces equivalent coverage to random seat
deployment schedules, the resonance structure is decorative.
F-5: If the slingshot package (structured summary for fresh instances) produces equivalent fresh-instance
performance to cold deployment (no summary), the slingshot is unnecessary.

10. Disclosed limitations
This methodology is a Tier 3 (predictive synthesis) construct. The orbital mapping is a structural parallel
between multi-body gravitational systems and multi-seat audit architectures. The parallel is grounded in shared
mathematical structure (both are dynamical systems with competing attractive/repulsive forces that require
specific configurations for stability), but the mapping has not been empirically validated. The methodology
enters as a PROPERTY at DETECTABLE, not as a proven discipline, per the admission protocol from the
concept document (Let_s_Go.md Section 3).
The Roche limit (R = 4) is estimated from the XZ Protocol's N=2 empirical observation (two seats developed
persistent HALTs after 3-4 consecutive audits). N=2 is not a reliable estimate. The true Roche limit likely varies
by model family, artifact type, and analytical framework. R = 4 is a conservative starting point, not a proven
constant.
The precession rate (90 degrees per HEO orbit) is chosen for mathematical convenience (4 frameworks, 4
orbits = 1 revolution). There is no empirical basis for this rate being optimal. Faster precession (more
frameworks per orbit) risks superficiality. Slower precession (fewer frameworks per orbit) risks framework
stagnation. The rate should be calibrated empirically.

0. Why quantum, not classical
The planetary orbital model (v0.1) solved three problems: tidal locking, cadence formalization, and framework
precession. But it left a structural gap: nothing in Newtonian mechanics prevents two planets from sharing the
same orbit. The model can describe a rotation schedule but cannot structurally prevent redundant deployment.

quantum state -- is not a guideline or a discipline. It is a structural law. When the audit architecture is modeled
on electron configurations rather than planetary orbits, redundancy elimination becomes a property of the
architecture itself. The architecture cannot be misconfigured because the misconfiguration is physically
forbidden.
The move from classical to quantum also adds: quantized energy levels (discrete analytical tiers, not
continuous distance), orbital shapes (distinct analytical geometries for each tier), a formal filling order (Aufbau -which seats to deploy first), a diversity-before-depth rule (Hund), shielding (inner shells reduce the analytical
burden on outer shells), and an emission spectrum (the finding-type fingerprint of any audit architecture).
